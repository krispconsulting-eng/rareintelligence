# Event blueprint: a two-day organisation intensive

One way to assemble the organisations course suite into a live event for the
people running or building rare disease patient organisations. It is a sample,
not a fixed timetable. The design choices matter more than the exact minutes:
mandatory pre-work, experiential over lecture, leadership and wellbeing woven
through, and a community of practice launched so capability spreads across the
sector.

## Before anyone arrives

- **Pre-work.** Course 1 (Governance, compliance and running an organisation)
  online modules are completed before the event. The room is not spent on
  compliance basics.
- **Spread, not concentrate.** Where capacity is tight, one place per
  organisation or region, so capability reaches more groups.
- **Materials.** Every group gets a take-home toolkit and access to the online
  academy and community of practice.

## Day one: foundations, funding and community

| Time | Session | Course | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | Welcome and governance pressure-test | 1 | Short plenary on the landscape, then a workshop testing each group's structure against compliance obligations |
| Morning | Funding and sustainability | 2 | Grant-readiness clinic and an industry-partnership session with the Code set first |
| Afternoon | Building and sustaining community | 3 | Codesign exercise, with members of priority populations in the room where possible |
| Afternoon | Moderation and risk | 3 | Real-scenario session on online community risk |
| Close | Distributed leadership check-in | 7 | Facilitated session |

## Day two: research readiness, investability and influence

| Time | Session | Course | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | Data assets and research readiness | 4 | Data-strategy breakout, then a governance and IP session that settles ownership before anything is built |
| Morning | Making the case | 5 | Pitch rehearsal with feedback |
| Afternoon | Systemic advocacy and submissions | 6 | Campaign-planning workshop and submission lab |
| Afternoon | Pitch session with expert judges | 5 | Groups present their case for partnership or investment and are pressure-tested |
| Close | Launching the community of practice | 6, 7 | Groups agree how to share the load across the sector, then the community launch |

Which of the two capstones closes the event depends on the cohort: the pitch
session for groups near research and industry, the ecosystem and
community-of-practice session for a support and advocacy focused cohort. Both can
run if the group is large enough to split.

## Why it is shaped this way

- **The clinics, breakouts and pitch are the point.** The grant clinic, the
  data-strategy and IP sessions, the submission lab and the pitch with judges are
  where organisational capability is actually built. The slides are scaffolding.
- **Leadership and wellbeing are not the closing thank-you.** They open and close
  each day, because an organisation that burns out its one committed person is
  not sustainable.
- **The event does not end when the room empties.** The community of practice,
  the alumni network and mentoring carry the learning across the sector, which is
  the difference between an event groups attended and a capability the sector
  keeps.

## Australian anchors used in the room

ACNC for governance and compliance, the Medicines Australia Code for the
industry-partnership and pitch sessions, TGA, PBAC and MSAC for the access and
evidence work, the National Strategic Action Plan for Rare Diseases as the
framework for the advocacy sessions, and the NHMRC Statement on Consumer and
Community Involvement for the research-readiness work. Any industry-supported
delivery carries Code of Conduct compliance and a conflict-of-interest approach
set at the design stage.
