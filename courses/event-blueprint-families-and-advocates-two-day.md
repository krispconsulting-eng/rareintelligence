# Event blueprint: a two-day face-to-face program

One way to assemble the course suite into a live event for families and
advocates. It is a sample, not a fixed timetable. The design choices matter more
than the exact minutes: mandatory pre-work, experiential over lecture, wellbeing
woven through, and a community launched so the value keeps compounding.

## Before anyone arrives

- **Pre-work.** Course 1 (Understanding the rare disease system) online modules
  are completed before the event. The room is not spent on definitions.
- **Access built in.** A funded carer place with accommodation where needed.
  Where capacity is tight, one place per family or region, so capability spreads
  rather than concentrates.
- **Materials.** Every participant gets a take-home toolkit and access to the
  online academy and community of practice.

## Day one: orientation and partnering

| Time | Session | Course | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | Welcome and "map your own pathway" | 1 | Short plenary, then facilitated exercise on each family's real diagnostic story |
| Morning | From lived experience to evidence | 2 | Co-design workshop |
| Afternoon | Inside research and trials: endpoints that matter | 3 | Hands-on workshop where advocates argue for their community's outcomes |
| Afternoon | Ethics committee, from the inside | 3 | Role-play simulation reviewing a real protocol |
| Close | Wellbeing check-in | 6 | Facilitated session |

A family and carer support stream (Course 6) runs in parallel across the day for
those who want it, so the whole family is supported, not only the person at the
table.

## Day two: access and influence

| Time | Session | Course | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | Access, regulation and HTA in Australia | 4 | Briefing, then a mini PBAC or MSAC simulation preparing and presenting a consumer input |
| Morning | Expert office hours | 4 | Twenty-minute one-to-one slots with people who have sat on the committee side |
| Afternoon | Advocacy that moves the system | 5 | Campaign-planning workshop and submission lab |
| Afternoon | How a decision actually gets made | 5 | Multi-stakeholder panel showing where an advocate can intervene |
| Close | Storytelling, and launching the community | 5, 6 | Facilitated storytelling session, then community-of-practice launch |

## Why it is shaped this way

- **The simulations and clinics are the point.** The ethics role-play, the PBAC
  or MSAC simulation, the office hours and the submission lab are where capability
  is actually built. The slides are scaffolding.
- **Wellbeing is not the closing thank-you.** It opens and closes each day and
  runs as a parallel family stream, because the work is emotionally demanding.
- **The event does not end when the room empties.** The community of practice,
  the alumni network and mentoring carry the learning forward, which is the
  difference between an event people attended and a capability a community keeps.

## Australian anchors used in the room

TGA for regulation, PBAC and MSAC for the reimbursement and HTA simulations, the
National Strategic Action Plan for Rare Diseases as the framework for the
advocacy sessions, and the NHMRC Statement on Consumer and Community Involvement
for the research-partnering work. Any industry-supported delivery carries Code of
Conduct compliance and a conflict-of-interest approach set at the design stage.
