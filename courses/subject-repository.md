# Subject Repository: Patient Organisation and Consumer Capability

A reusable catalogue for designing a future training event. This is the source
menu. The built suite for families and advocates is in
`course-suite-families-and-advocates.md`.

## Purpose

This repository distils the recurring subjects across the major rare disease
patient-training programs worldwide into one menu you can select, sequence and
source from when designing an event. It draws on the EURORDIS Open Academy
schools (Medicines R&D; Scientific Innovation & Translational Research), the
EUPATI Open Classroom, EUCAPA, the Global Genes RARE Drug Development Symposium,
Genetic Alliance UK, Rare Voices Australia (RARE Portal and RArEST), the Patient
Voice Initiative, and the Australian consumer-and-community-involvement
infrastructure.

Subjects are grouped into ten domains. For each subject you get a one-line
scope, the primary audience, the delivery formats that have been used for it in
practice, and the programs it is drawn from, so you can see where it is proven
and where to find material or faculty.

## How to use it

Select by audience and goal, not by domain order. Most events need subjects from
several domains. Use the audience tag to keep the level right: a session aimed at
organisation leaders is wasted on individual consumers preparing for an HTA
committee, and vice versa. Use the format column to balance the agenda between
input (plenary, e-learning) and application (breakouts, simulations, office
hours). Use the source tags to decide what to license or adapt rather than build
from scratch.

**Audience key.** ORG = patient organisation capability (people running or
building a group). ADV = individual advocate or consumer acting as a research,
regulatory or HTA partner. BOTH = relevant to either, usually at different
depths. RES = designed for researchers/clinicians (included because the content
transfers).

**Source key.** OA-MRD = EURORDIS School on Medicines R&D. OA-SITR = EURORDIS
School on Scientific Innovation & Translational Research. EUPATI = EUPATI Open
Classroom (incl. HTAR course). EUCAPA = European Capacity Building for Patients.
RDDS = Global Genes RARE Drug Development Symposium. GAUK = Genetic Alliance UK.
RVA = Rare Voices Australia. PVI = Patient Voice Initiative. CCI-AU = Australian
consumer-and-community-involvement programs (CCIP, AHRA, ACTA, Involve
Australia, NHMRC Statement).

**Jurisdiction note.** Where a subject names a regulator, HTA body or policy
(EMA, FDA, EU HTAR, Medicaid), swap in the Australian equivalent for an
Australian event: TGA for regulation, PBAC and MSAC for reimbursement and HTA,
the National Strategic Action Plan for Rare Diseases for policy, the NHMRC
Statement on Consumer and Community Involvement for research involvement, and the
Medicines Australia Code of Conduct for any industry-facing content.

## Delivery-format toolkit

The programs converge on a small set of formats. Each does a particular job; a
good agenda mixes input with application.

- **Pre-event e-learning (self-paced):** levels a mixed-ability cohort before
  anyone meets. The EURORDIS schools and EUPATI both depend on it.
- **Live pre-event webinars with a completion threshold:** keeps people engaged
  across a blended pathway. EURORDIS requires attendance at a minimum number to
  certify.
- **Plenary, keynote or fireside chat:** sets framing and ambition. Best kept
  short.
- **Multi-stakeholder panel:** shows tension and trade-offs between regulator,
  payer, industry and patient.
- **Interactive breakouts (by topic or disease):** the workhorse for
  application; used after almost every RDDS and EURORDIS main session.
- **Role-play simulations:** the highest-impact regulatory and ethics learning.
  EURORDIS runs an ethics-committee role-play and a mini-COMP/SAWP regulatory
  simulation.
- **Hands-on design workshops:** participants build something, such as a
  clinical development plan.
- **Case-scenario discussions:** real disease case studies (Angelman,
  Niemann-Pick Type C at RDDS; secondary-findings scenarios at EURORDIS).
- **Expert office hours (one-to-one, ~20 minutes):** high personalisation at low
  cost; an RDDS signature.
- **Research or site visits:** connect classroom to practice (a children's
  hospital; a genomics centre).
- **Pitch sessions with expert judges:** force investment-readiness thinking;
  the RDDS finale.
- **Communities of practice:** sustained peer learning between events (RVA's
  ARDO-CoP).
- **Mentoring programs:** small mentor-to-mentee groups plus masterclasses
  (EURORDIS alumni mentoring).
- **Alumni network:** keeps value compounding long after the room empties.
- **Take-home toolkits and resource libraries:** extend reach to those who could
  not attend.

## Signature design features worth adopting

- **A blended pathway.** Mandatory pre-work means the in-person time is spent on
  application, not basics.
- **A two-audience cohort.** EURORDIS deliberately mixes advocates with
  early-career researchers so each learns the other's world. Consider mixing
  consumers with clinicians or HTA staff.
- **One place per organisation or region.** EURORDIS selects one applicant per
  country per school to spread capability rather than concentrate it.
- **A funded carer place.** EURORDIS covers accommodation for a registered carer
  where needed. Real access, not just a fee waiver.
- **Experiential over lecture.** The strongest sessions are simulations, pitches
  and breakouts, not slides.
- **Tiered commitment.** EUCAPA offers a two-hour introduction, an eight-hour
  fast-track and a four-day extended course, so people enter at the depth they
  can manage.
- **A persistent community.** Community of practice, alumni network and mentoring
  turn a one-off event into a capability that grows.
- **Wellbeing built in, not bolted on.** Genetic Alliance UK treats resilience
  as core content because the work is emotionally demanding.
- **Free, multilingual e-learning.** Reaches the people who will never be in the
  room.

## Starter configurations

Four ways to assemble the repository into an event, depending on who you are
training.

**1. Patient-organisation intensive (two days, ORG).** Domains A, G, H and J,
with a wellbeing strand from I. Formats: plenary framing, breakouts by
capability, expert office hours, and a community-of-practice launch so it
continues. Outcome: stronger, more sustainable organisations.

**2. Consumer HTA fast-track (blended, ADV).** Domain D as the spine, with
supporting pieces from C (endpoints, real-world evidence) and E (patient-expert
role, independence). EUCAPA-style tiered delivery: e-learning pre-work, one
intensive day, then mentoring. Outcome: consumers ready to sit on or contribute
to HTA processes.

**3. Research-partner school (three to four days, BOTH).** Domains B, C and E,
with leadership from I. EURORDIS-style blended pathway: e-learning, live
webinars, in-person days, a site visit, and entry to an alumni network. Outcome:
advocates who can partner credibly across the research lifecycle.

**4. Make-your-program-investable workshop (one to two days, ORG).** Domain B
(data and natural history), C (trial readiness), F (data, IP, publication) and G
(investment readiness), culminating in a pitch session with expert judges.
RDDS-style. Outcome: communities that can attract and sustain industry and
investor interest.

## Caveats

- This is a menu synthesised from existing programs, not an endorsement of any
  one curriculum. Select for your audience and jurisdiction.
- Several subjects are jurisdiction-specific. Swap EU and US anchors (EMA, FDA,
  EU HTAR, Medicaid) for Australian equivalents (TGA, PBAC, MSAC, the National
  Strategic Action Plan, the NHMRC CCI Statement, the Medicines Australia Code).
- Source tags show where a subject is proven and where material exists, not the
  only place to source it. Faculty and effort vary widely by subject.
- Depth matters more than coverage. A short event that does six subjects well
  beats one that names twenty. Use the audience tags to avoid pitching
  organisational content at individual consumers, or vice versa.
- For any industry-supported event, build in Code of Conduct compliance and a
  clear conflict-of-interest approach from the design stage, not as an
  afterthought.
